# Macronutrients, Caloric Balance, and Sports Nutrition

## Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) and Caloric Balance
Energy balance governs changes in body composition. Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) comprises Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR ~60-70%), Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (NEAT ~15%), Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (EAT ~5-10%), and Thermic Effect of Food (TEF ~10%).
- For Lean Mass Hypertrophy: Maintain a moderate caloric surplus of 250 to 450 kcal/day above maintenance (approx +10-15% surplus) to maximize muscle protein synthesis while minimizing adipose fat accumulation.
- For Fat Loss & Definition: Implement a moderate caloric deficit of 350 to 500 kcal/day (approx 15-20% deficit) with high dietary protein to spare lean muscle tissue breakdown.

## Protein Synthesis and Daily Target Requirements
Dietary protein supplies the essential amino acids (specifically leucine) required to trigger the mTOR pathway for muscle protein synthesis (MPS). 
Active individuals and strength athletes should target 1.6 to 2.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day (0.75 - 1.0 g/lb). 
Distribute protein intake evenly across 3 to 5 meals per day (25-40g protein per meal containing at least 2.5g leucine) to repeatedly stimulate muscle protein synthesis throughout the 24-hour circadian cycle.

## Carbohydrates and Healthy Dietary Fats
Carbohydrates are the primary high-yield metabolic fuel for anaerobic glycolysis and high-intensity cardiovascular training. 
Endurance and power athletes should consume 3 to 7 grams of carbohydrates per kilogram of body weight daily depending on training volume. 
Dietary fats should constitute 20% to 35% of total caloric intake (0.7-1.0 g/kg), prioritizing monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fats (omega-3 fatty acids EPA/DHA) to support endocrine steroid hormone production (testosterone, cortisol regulation) and reduce systemic joint inflammation.
