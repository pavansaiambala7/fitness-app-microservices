# Hydration Dynamics, Electrolyte Balance, and Athletic Micronutrients

## Hydration Protocols for Performance
Dehydration exceeding a 2% loss in body weight significantly impairs aerobic capacity, elevates core body temperature, increases heart rate by 4-8 bpm at a given workload, and degrades cognitive decision-making.
- Pre-Exercise Hydration: Ingest 400 to 600 ml (14-20 oz) of water 2 to 3 hours prior to training, followed by 200 to 300 ml 15-20 minutes before warmup.
- Intra-Exercise Fluid Replacement: Sip 150 to 250 ml every 15 to 20 minutes during prolonged exertion (>45 mins).
- Post-Exercise Rehydration Formula: Consume 1.25 to 1.5 liters of fluid for every 1 kilogram of body weight lost through sweat during the session within 2 to 4 hours post-workout.

## Electrolyte Balance: Sodium, Potassium, and Magnesium
Heavy sweating depletes critical plasma electrolytes necessary for neuromuscular transmission and muscle contraction.
- Sodium (Na+): The primary extracellular cation lost in sweat (approx 800-1500mg per liter of sweat). Sodium stimulates thirst, promotes fluid retention in the vascular space, and prevents hyponatremia.
- Potassium (K+): Intracellular cation crucial for muscle cell membrane potential and preventing post-workout muscular spasms. Include potassium-rich foods (avocados, bananas, spinach, coconut water).
- Magnesium (Mg2+): Cofactor for over 300 enzymatic reactions, including ATP energy production and muscle relaxation. Supplementing 200-400mg magnesium glycinate before sleep improves sleep quality and reduces muscle cramping.

## Critical Micronutrients for Athletic Recovery
1. Vitamin D3 & K2: Regulates calcium homeostasis, bone mineral density, and modulates systemic immune resistance against upper respiratory tract infections.
2. Zinc: Supports cellular repair, protein synthesis, and maintenance of healthy baseline serum testosterone levels.
3. Antioxidants (Vitamins C & E, Polyphenols): Moderate exercise-induced oxidative stress without blunting natural mitochondrial adaptive signaling.
