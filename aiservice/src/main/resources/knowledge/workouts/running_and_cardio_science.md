# Running Science & Aerobic Endurance Mechanics

## Zone 2 Aerobic Base Training
Zone 2 aerobic endurance running is defined as exertion at 60% to 70% of maximum heart rate (HR max = 220 - age). 
Training extensively in Zone 2 triggers mitochondrial biogenesis, enhances capillary density in slow-twitch muscle fibers, and trains the body to utilize free fatty acids as the primary energy substrate via beta-oxidation instead of rapidly depleting glycogen stores. 
During Zone 2 workouts, athletes must maintain a conversational pace where complete sentences can be spoken without gasping for breath.

## Lactate Threshold & High-Intensity Cardio
High-intensity tempo runs and interval sprints exceeding 85% of HR max push metabolic demand past the anaerobic threshold. 
Lactic acid accumulates faster than clearance mechanisms can buffer hydrogen ions, leading to muscular fatigue and drop in mechanical efficiency. 
High-intensity sessions should be limited to 20% of total weekly cardiovascular volume (the 80/20 polarized training model). 
A mandatory recovery window of 48 to 72 hours must be observed between maximal effort sprint or tempo sessions to avoid chronic sympathetic nervous system overload.

## Running Biomechanics and Cadence Optimization
Optimal running cadence for distance runners is between 170 and 185 strides per minute (SPM). 
Maintaining high cadence prevents overstriding—a common mechanical flaw where the foot strikes excessively ahead of the center of mass, producing high ground braking forces and elevating knee and hip joint stress. 
Midfoot striking under a slightly forward-tilted torso minimizes vertical oscillation and maximizes kinetic energy return through the Achilles tendon elastic spring mechanism.
